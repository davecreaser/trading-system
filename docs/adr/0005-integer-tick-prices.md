# Integer ticks instead of floating-point prices

Prices could be represented as `double` (matches how a price like $10.05 is naturally thought of) or as an integer count of the instrument's minimum tick size (e.g. $10.05 at a $0.01 tick becomes the integer 1005). We chose integer ticks. Price-time priority depends on comparing prices for exact equality constantly — deciding whether two orders sit at the "same" price level — and floating-point cannot represent most decimal fractions exactly, making equality comparisons an entire category of subtle, hard-to-debug bugs waiting to happen (two orders that are "the same price" landing in different price levels, for instance). This is also what real exchanges and trading systems actually do, not just a defensive workaround. The cost is a small amount of upfront conversion logic (real-world price to tick, and back for display) instead of using prices as-is.
