# OrderReplaced never produces a shadow fill, only a cancel-like depth reduction

The strategy's shadow-fill simulation (ADR-0009) tracks, per side, how much real depth is ahead of its hypothetical order and distinguishes depth leaving via a trade (fillable) from depth leaving via a cancel (never fillable). `OrderReplaced` is structurally dual: the replaced order's *old* price loses its resting quantity (a cancel-like consequence, always on the replaced order's own side, since NASDAQ's own spec confirms side can't change on a replace), while its *new*, possibly more aggressive price could cross the spread (a trade-like consequence, but only ever on the *opposite* side). In principle those two consequences could be told apart correctly — but the message itself carries no side field, and the strategy deliberately doesn't duplicate ITCH-reference-number-to-side tracking elsewhere in the system (the same reasoning ADR-0009 already used to justify reusing `OrderBook`'s own state instead of re-deriving it). Without that information, there's no reliable way to tell which of the strategy's two trackers (bid or ask) is looking at the cancel-like consequence versus the trade-like one for any given `OrderReplaced`.

We decided to treat every `OrderReplaced`-driven depth decrease as cancel-like on both trackers, never letting it produce a fill — the same treatment as `OrderDelete`/`OrderCancelled`.

**Consequences**: some genuine fills that happen to arrive via a Replace (rather than a fresh `AddOrder`) will be silently missed, undercounting real fills in a rare case. This is deliberate: inventing a fill that never really reached the strategy would corrupt `current_inventory_` and everything Epic 4 computes from it — a strictly worse failure mode than conservative undercounting.
